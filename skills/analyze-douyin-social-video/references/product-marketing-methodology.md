# Product Marketing video methodology

Use this reference after classification and evidence collection. Keep the reasoning specific to the video; fixed questions are allowed, fixed conclusions are not.

## Contents

1. Shared six-part reasoning sequence
2. Evidence levels
3. Type-specific lenses
4. Three-paragraph report output
5. Positive-only and evidence checks

## Shared six-part reasoning sequence

### 1. Observed creator assets and Hook

Identify the creator assets that are demonstrated in this video, then identify how the opening earns attention:

- creator assets: established identity, relationship, professional access, character IP, performance skill, explanation skill, trial behavior, or recurring format;
- mechanism: emotional or topical resonance, visual contrast, curiosity or challenge, decision question, conflict, or reversal;
- target circle: an emotion-led audience, a problem-led audience, or both;
- familiar motif: the quickly recognizable content frame, life stage, relationship, or category question;
- distinctive angle: the person, circumstance, tension, test design, or novelty that makes this video specific;
- narrative device: role play, identity contrast, repeated check-in, experiment, comparison, reveal, escalation, or reversal.

Describe assets the current creator actually shows. Do not turn this into a recommendation for a hypothetical future KOL. Anchor Hook evidence at the opening. Describe a retention mechanism or viewer payoff; never claim actual completion rate without performance data.

### 2. Situated scene and consumer tension

Define the audience, occasion, and need in the video's own situation:

- who is in the situation;
- when, where, and under what condition the need appears;
- what practical, emotional, identity, or social tension the viewer recognizes;
- how the situation turns a broad skincare concern into a concrete job.

Prefer `wedding preparation before a high-expectation appearance` over `anti-aging need`, or `parents preparing for frequent outdoor time with children` over `sun protection`.

### 3. Product entry and creator fit

Define the product's positive content identity: evaluation subject, routine enabler, scene companion, character solution, secret weapon, gift, travel kit, status recovery device, or conversion offer.

Explain:

- the bridge from the existing content line to product use;
- why the identity fits the creator's established expertise, relationship, role, or lifestyle;
- what the product enables the person, routine, or story to continue, complete, protect, or recover;
- the concrete reason the product belongs at that exact point in the content, such as relationship care, routine continuity, credible trial, humor, plot consequence, or narrative payoff.

### 4. Selling-point strategy and RTB persuasion chain

Commit to one core product perception and one core selling point before organizing reasons to believe. Distinguish:

- **Core selling point**: the one message the viewer should retain.
- **Supporting points**: only the claims needed to explain or reinforce the core selling point.
- **Strongest RTB**: the single piece of evidence that does the most persuasive work for this selling point.
- **Deprioritized mentions**: ingredients, textures, fragrances, claims, or actions that appear in the video but do not strengthen the chosen core perception. Keep these in the internal audit field and omit them from the report prose.

1. **Sensoriality**: texture, dispensing, spread, absorption, finish, comfort, immediate appearance, or another physical usage cue.
2. **Scientific language**: ingredient, technology, specification, or mechanism, plus the creator's plain-language translation of why it matters.
3. **Proof**: repeated use, time-separated check-ins, comparison, instrument reading, before/after state, verified external support, or clearly attributed reputation.

Choose the strongest RTB by fit with the claimed selling point, not by a universal ranking. A visible texture demonstration may be strongest for a lightweight-finish claim but insufficient for long-term efficacy. Repeated use or a time-separated check-in may be persuasive for durability but still remains attributed user feedback unless independently verified. State why the selected RTB persuades. Do not treat the presence of an ingredient as proof of efficacy.

### 5. CTA and purchase-barrier reduction

When a CTA exists, classify its role:

- `threshold_reduction`: lowers price, trial-size, effort, or decision risk;
- `scene_summary`: tells the viewer when or for whom the product is useful;
- `emotional_permission`: makes the purchase feel deserved, caring, identity-consistent, or gift-worthy;
- `direct_action`: gives an explicit cart, live-room, store, or purchase instruction.

Explain the purchase barrier the CTA reduces. If no CTA is present, keep the internal CTA block empty and omit CTA commentary from the report.

### 6. Core effective mechanism and observed integration chain

Explain this video's actual execution:

- where the content begins;
- which tension or need makes the product relevant;
- how the product enters;
- which one mechanism does the main work, such as relationship care, professional credibility, routine continuity, plot consequence, or test design;
- how demonstration or proof completes persuasion;
- how the video returns to or resolves its content line;
- one observed three-to-five-node integration chain.

The chain label alone is insufficient: explain why the sequence works. Write in the present or past tense about the current creator and video. Do not default to future content-territory, hypothetical KOL, media, budget, or performance recommendations. The report should state only successful mechanisms and should not critique the video.

## Evidence levels

Label every RTB item with one evidence type:

- `observed_action`: directly visible product handling, application, texture, finish, or state;
- `spoken_claim`: a creator or subtitle claim;
- `user_feedback`: a user's stated subjective experience;
- `displayed_data`: a number, instrument result, label, or comparison displayed in the video;
- `external_proof`: support independently verified outside the creator's statement;
- `analyst_inference`: a cautious interpretation derived from observed context.

Keep these levels distinct. Attribute creator claims and subjective feedback. A displayed number proves only that the video displayed that number unless its methodology is independently verified.

## Type-specific lenses

### SEEDING

The product or purchase decision is the main line. Determine:

- the opening question, pain point, barrier, comparison, or test design;
- whether the creator moves from sensorial demonstration to scientific explanation or stronger proof;
- the claim hierarchy and the strongest RTB;
- the credibility source: expertise, repeated use, time-separated check-in, test, comparison, identity, or category experience;
- the product perception and purchase barrier reduced.

For creator assets, record category authority, credible trial behavior, clear explanation, and audience relevance demonstrated in the current video.

### LIFESTYLE

The person, life, or scene is the main line. Determine:

- the familiar lifestyle motif and the creator's distinctive identity or circumstance;
- the environmental, routine, relationship, or life-stage condition that creates the need;
- the product's identity inside the routine;
- how the use action preserves continuity with the life story;
- why the creator's established life and audience make the use believable.

For creator assets, record stable identity, authentic access to the scene, audience identification, and native product use demonstrated in the current video.

### DRAMA

Plot, character conflict, or emotional payoff is the main line. Determine:

- the social tension, relationship conflict, or cultural topic;
- the audience condition represented by the plot, beyond literal plot details;
- the narrative devices that create payoff: role play, identity contrast, escalation, dense jokes, reveal, or reversal;
- how a plot consequence becomes a concrete skincare state;
- the product's character or story function;
- how humor, role consistency, or payoff makes the product entry coherent within the plot.

For creator assets, record existing character IP, social-topic credibility, scripting, acting, and native integration behavior demonstrated in the current video.

### BRANDING

Determine the brand proposition, symbolic asset, cultural frame, or campaign idea; the audience identity it affirms; and how product or brand proof supports that meaning. Explain the observed brand-to-content mechanism rather than proposing a future campaign.

### OFFER

Determine the conversion occasion, urgency or benefit mechanism, purchase barrier, claim hierarchy, and CTA. Explain how credible demonstration and clear action preserve the creator's normal content logic.

### OTHERS

Do not invent product reasoning. Keep unavailable product-specific internal fields empty and state only supported content observations.

## Judgment before prose

The completed analysis fields are evidence inputs, not a writing checklist. Before drafting, commit to:

1. one demonstrated creator asset;
2. one familiar theme and one distinctive angle;
3. one viewer payoff;
4. one situated product role and one core selling point;
5. one strongest RTB and the belief it supports;
6. one reason the product belongs at this point in the video;
7. one observed three-to-five-node integration chain.

Choose a hierarchy before writing. If the answer still fits after replacing the creator, situation, and product with unrelated ones, rebuild it from the timestamped evidence.

## Three-paragraph report output

Write the section title as `Product Marketing 洞察` and output exactly three paragraphs:

1. **内容价值**: use `博主资产 + 熟悉主题 + 独特视角 + 观看价值`. Start with the creator-specific identity, access, relationship, performance skill, or recording capability demonstrated in the video. Then connect the familiar theme and distinctive angle to the emotional or practical payoff. Do not retell the chronology.
2. **产品认知**: use `场景化产品角色 + 一个核心卖点 + 需求证据 + 主要使用证明`. State what the product becomes in this exact situation, then establish the need and select the one proof that does the most persuasive work. Keep ingredients, texture, actions, and feedback subordinate to the retained perception.
3. **营销启示**: use `需求如何产生 + 产品为什么在这里出现 + 什么证据完成说服 + 植入链路`. Explain the causal mechanism before naming the three-to-five-node chain. Analyze the current video without using external-review language such as `竞品`, `品牌应`, or `可借鉴`.

Assign information to one primary paragraph:

- creator asset, theme, angle, and viewer payoff → `内容价值`;
- need, product role, selling point, and primary proof → `产品认知`;
- entry reason, persuasive function, and causal chain → `营销启示`.

Do not repeat a complete fact, action list, or conclusion across paragraphs. A later paragraph may refer to an earlier fact only when it adds a new causal meaning. Delete any sentence whose meaning is already complete elsewhere.

Use evidence as `fact → meaning`, not as an inventory. For example, `17 小时执勤` matters because it establishes sustained environmental depletion; `双颊湿敷` matters because it visibly demonstrates the proposed recovery method.

Add one or two `potential_topics` hashtag strings for every non-`OTHERS` video. Base them on the video's observed scene, relationship, or consumer concern, use natural audience language, and never describe them as verified platform hotspots. Keep the tags outside `activation_implication.text`; the report renderer appends them to the third paragraph as `潜力话题：#标签一 #标签二`.

Stop when the supported reasoning is complete. Do not add or remove prose to meet a character count.
Store paragraph prose without repeating `内容价值：`, `产品认知：`, or `营销启示：`; the PDF renderer supplies those labels.

## Positive-only and evidence checks

- Attach at least one timestamped transcript or visual item to each paragraph.
- Write only strengths, successful mechanisms, and reusable value. Do not add weaknesses, negative reviews, failure analysis, or `what not to copy`.
- If evidence is absent, omit the conclusion instead of criticizing the video.
- Keep observed facts, creator claims, user feedback, displayed data, external proof, and analyst inference distinct.
- Use video-specific nouns: situation, character, action, claim, ingredient, device, result, or CTA.
- Do not mistake specificity for detail volume. Select only details that establish viewing value, consumer need, recommendation legitimacy, product belief, or narrative resolution.
- Avoid unsupported analyst shorthand such as `形成闭环`, `落到可见场景`, `完成说服`, or `建立认知`. These phrases are acceptable only when the same sentence names the concrete cause, proof, and resulting belief.
- Do not call a topic a trend or hotspot from one video alone. Without external corroboration, use `常见内容母题`, `具有讨论度`, or equivalent cautious language.
- Do not evaluate actual audience agreement, completion, traffic, sharing, clicks, conversion, sales, budget, or media allocation. Those belong to a separate performance-data workflow.
- Explain product indispensability positively by stating what it enables in the content.
- State one complete causal mechanism and one three-to-five-node chain. Do not use a chain label without explaining why the sequence works.
- Avoid external-review or future-planning phrases in `营销启示`, including `竞品`, `品牌应`, `可借鉴`, `适合布局`, `适合由`, `适合与`, `由某类 KOL 承载`, `建议复用`, `选择优质达人`, or unsupported claims about reducing advertising resistance.
- Use `references/product-marketing-examples.md` as a reasoning guide, not as copy-ready wording.

## Product-name display rule

- Only in the three analyst-authored paragraphs of section 3, `Product Marketing 洞察`, replace every specific product name with `产品`.
- Preserve exact product names everywhere else, including evidence, transcript, product-ad details, screenshot captions, and audit fields.
- Do not alter screenshot pixels. Visible names inside evidence images remain original.
